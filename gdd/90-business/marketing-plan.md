# Marketing plan

> [!info] Source
> `marketing/PLAN.md`. Summarized here; that file is the working document.

**Goal: 8,000 Steam wishlists at launch, under 3 hours per week of the user's time, with an agent pipeline carrying volume.** Success measured in wishlists and revenue, not followers.

## Where it starts from

Kernel Panic is far past prototype: a 10-day run, 27 tickets plus a finale, 12 named customers, 18 augments, a complete story with a real ending, a custom audio engine, and a 200-seed balance harness.

What does not exist is any commercial surface. The `twitter:site` meta tag currently credits `@Higgsfield`, so every link shared today promotes the hosting platform.

> [!warning] The whole game is free and public
> Named in the plan as the single biggest commercial problem, and something that must be fixed **before any marketing spend**. See [[monetization]].

## Two research findings that invert the obvious plan

**1. Steam ranks on conversion, not impressions.** The Q2 2026 discovery refresh weights page-visit-to-wishlist conversion over raw impressions. A page converting 4% is treated meaningfully better than one at 1.5% with similar impressions.

So **unqualified traffic actively damages you**: it drives conversion down and the algorithm demotes you for it. Broad reach is a liability, and every channel decision is filtered for audience qualification rather than volume.

**2. Next Fest amplifies momentum, it cannot create it.** The strongest correlation in the February 2026 data is between pre-fest wishlist count and fest earnings: Spearman **0.825**.

You get **one Next Fest per game**:

| | Oct 2026 | Feb to Mar 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Wishlists going in | ~0 to 500 | 4,000 to 6,000 |
| Realistic wishlists out | 300 to 800 | 3,000 to 8,000 |
| Store page age | 3 to 6 weeks | 5 to 6 months |
| Demo maturity | rushed | tested through 2 themed fests |

Same one-shot ticket, roughly **10x the return for waiting four months**.
